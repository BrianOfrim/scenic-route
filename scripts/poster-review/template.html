The model’s verdicts stay hidden during review. You can change any rating until you finish.

All three criteria start at Pass. Override failures and use Save & next to confirm each poster; on the last poster, use Save assessment. Unconfirmed defaults stay out of comparisons. Compare assessed reveals only saved assessments; remaining posters stay editable with judge verdicts hidden.

Your ratings vs. the judge

False accept: judge passed, you failed. False reject: judge failed, you passed. Uncertain, not-applicable and errored model responses are counted separately and excluded from binary agreement.

This small trial is not a representative accuracy benchmark. You can revise any assessment after reveal. Results use your latest ratings; earlier revisions are preserved in local history. Export the ratings to retain a portable copy.

Where you and the judge diverge

How to align the judge with your preferences

1. Adjudicate the disagreement. Inspect the poster and references, then decide whether your assessment stands, the judge was right, or the case needs another look. Describe the exact visual evidence and your tolerance for stylization.

2. Write a reusable rule. Explain what should pass or fail on future posters. For example: “Simplified shorelines are acceptable if the inlet positions and relative sizes remain recognizable.” Save paired pass/fail examples near the threshold.

3. Calibrate the prompt, then measure. Export reviewed feedback to revise the rubric and select examples. Keep agreeing cases as regression checks. Validate the revised judge on new route families; style variants of the same route are closely related. This set is useful for calibration, but cannot establish general accuracy by itself. Freeze the adjudicated labels before comparing prompt versions; changing human labels alone does not improve the judge.

Feedback is saved locally. Accepting the judge explicitly updates your rating; the original disagreement remains in this queue and revision history. Exported rules are proposals until incorporated into a new judge prompt.
